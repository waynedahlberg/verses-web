A chapter is a useful map. It is a poor place to stop and look. When the whole passage is on screen, the eye keeps traveling, and the verse you meant to hear goes by with it.

Reading one verse at a time is a way of refusing that slide.

## What changes

The words get heavier. A conjunction matters. You notice who is speaking, and who they are speaking to, because nothing below the verse is competing for the next scroll.

That is not a slower personality. It is a smaller frame. The same passage is still there when you are ready for the next line.

If you want the longer argument for why we are building this, it is in [why we are building Verses](/blog/why-we-are-building-verses).
